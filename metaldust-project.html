Executive Summary

Grinding operations at Arrem Industries, Inc. generate fine metal dust that presents severe health hazards, accelerates equipment wear, and creates workspace slip hazards. This capstone project engineers a comprehensive metal dust control system to isolate workstations, capture particulates at the source, and ensure strict compliance with OSH and Philippine Mechanical Code standards. Through rigorous Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) modeling and multi-criteria decision analysis, the localized vacuum architecture (Alternative 2) was selected to maximize capture efficiency and long-term operational reliability.

Problem Statement

Fine metal dust generated during industrial grinding and alloy machining impairs pulmonary surfactant function, contributing to chronic respiratory illnesses among workers. Furthermore, particle accumulation inside machinery causes component clogging and jamming, leading to reduced operational efficiency and increased maintenance needs, while dust on facility floors creates hazardous slip conditions. Uncontrolled dispersion also risks non-compliance with environmental air quality regulations.

Project Background & Context

Established in 2000 at the Light Industry and Science Park 1 in Cabuyao, Laguna, Arrem Industries, Inc. manufactures custom sheet metal hardware for international brands like Komatsu and Hitachi. While utilizing modern CNC machinery, the facility's manual grinding processes remain a primary source of hazardous airborne and settled metal particulates. This project provides an engineered ventilation and capture framework to safeguard worker health and enhance equipment longevity.

Objectives

  • Design an integrated metal dust control system that isolates grinding operations and captures particulate matter at the source.
  • Eliminate manual collection methods through automated containment and localized exhaust mechanisms.
  • Align ventilation parameters with the Philippine Mechanical Code, ASHRAE, and OSH standards.
  • Utilize SolidWorks and Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) analysis to validate airflow behavior and capture performance.

Design Constraints & Criteria

  • Health and Safety (Rank 1 - 50% Weight): Prioritizes worker protection, stringent air quality control, and minimization of respiratory hazards.
  • Sustainability (Rank 2 - 33% Weight): Focuses on lifecycle impact, maintenance frequency, and long-term operational viability.
  • Environmental (Rank 3 - 17% Weight): Regulates energy consumption, general ventilation balance, and responsible material usage.

Design Alternative 1

Centralized Airflow System: Utilizes a 96-inch ceiling industrial fan (5,000 CFM, 10 HP) combined with six side air vents to push dust downward and corral particulates into a central water-filled floor gutter.

  • Pros: Comprehensive floor coverage with zero dead zones; permanent water trap isolation.
  • Cons: Higher energy consumption, complex airflow balancing, and greater maintenance overhead.

Design Alternative 2 (Selected)

Localized Vacuum System: Features a radial centrifugal fan (4,500 CFM, 10 HP) connected to dedicated rectangular vacuum drains positioned directly beneath each workstation, capturing heavy metal chips instantly at the source.

  • Pros: Immediate source capture, simpler monitoring, lower energy consumption, and cleaner individual workstations.
  • Cons: Requires precise alignment of local extraction hoods with grinding tools.

Selected Final Design

Design Alternative 2 (Localized Vacuum System) was chosen as the optimal system architecture. By targeting extraction directly beneath each grinding table, the system achieves superior capture efficiency, reduces auxiliary fan power requirements, and maintains exceptional stability across sensitivity analyses.

Methodology & Tools (e.g., SolidWorks, CFD Analysis, calculations)

  • SolidWorks CAD: Utilized for 3D modeling of workstation enclosures, duct networks, and spatial factory layouts.
  • Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD): Deployed to simulate internal velocity streamlines, pressure drops, and particulate trajectory distributions.
  • Mathematical Modeling: Applied specialized engineering equations for duct outlet velocity ($36.72\text{ m/s}$), fan power ($1.22\text{ kW}$), and dust transport mass ($18.72\text{ kg/hr}$).

Results & Performance Validation

CFD flow trajectory simulations confirmed uniform downward air movement and high-velocity suction directly at the tool interface. The selected configuration delivers an operational blower power requirement of **1.22 kW** at a system resistance of **1,500 Pa**, effectively transporting an estimated **18.72 kg of metal dust per hour** into the collection hopper.

Conclusions & Recommendations

The localized vacuum design successfully resolves dust accumulation challenges at Arrem Industries, Inc., providing a safer environment and protecting machinery. Future work should involve constructing a physical prototype for empirical testing, verifying long-term material durability against abrasive metal slurries, and conducting a complete economic cost-benefit analysis.
