Executive Summary

Agricultural sectors face severe post-harvest losses due to weather unpredictability, high ambient humidity, and inefficient drying options[cite: 2]. REZIDAC is an engineered hybrid biomass-solar agricultural dryer integrated with a 5 kg solid-state zeolite adsorption bed for precision humidity regulation[cite: 2]. This project transforms raw agricultural thermodynamic requirements into a robust mechanical system, stabilizing drying parameters, preventing product over-drying, and achieving a calculated 36.2% ROI within the first year[cite: 2].

Problem Statement

Traditional drying approaches (such as open-sun drying) leave agricultural yields heavily exposed to sudden precipitation, environmental contaminants, and uncontrolled moisture fluctuations leading to spoilage or over-drying. Conventional mechanical dryers often suffer from excessive specific energy consumption and heavy reliance on continuous fossil fuels or grid power. A resilient, cost-effective hybrid drying mechanism is required to maintain consistent thermal output and relative humidity control regardless of ambient weather shifts.

Project Background & Context

During peak harvesting seasons, local farmers experience heavy economic losses because open-sun drying leaves crops susceptible to sudden rain, contamination, and fluctuating humidity[cite: 2]. REZIDAC was conceptualized to provide a resilient, localized, and energy-efficient post-harvest processing unit that blends renewable solar thermal energy with reliable backup biomass combustion[cite: 2].

Objectives

  • Design a hybrid biomass-solar agricultural dryer to mitigate weather dependency[cite: 2].
  • Integrate a solid-state zeolite desiccant bed to dynamically regulate humidity and eliminate moisture spikes[cite: 2].
  • Validate thermal efficiency, fluid behavior, and structural integrity through advanced CFD simulations[cite: 2].
  • Ensure strong economic viability with a rapid payback period and high return on investment[cite: 2].

Design Constraints & Criteria

  • Thermal Stability: Must maintain a steady drying temperature around 45°C despite ambient meteorological shifts[cite: 2].
  • Material Availability: Must utilize locally accessible fabrication components to support low-cost manufacturing[cite: 2].
  • Psychrometric Criteria: Air velocity and vapor pressure deficits must be controlled to prevent crop case-hardening.

Design Alternative 1

Direct-Mode Open Solar Dryer: A purely solar-driven configuration utilizing flat-plate collectors to direct ambient air straight into the drying chamber without supplementary heaters.

  • Pros: Zero fuel cost; simple architecture.
  • Cons: Entirely vulnerable to cloud cover, rain, and nocturnal weather changes.

Design Alternative 2

Standalone Biomass-Fired Forced Convection Dryer: A system powered exclusively by a biomass combustion furnace and a forced convection blower.

  • Pros: Complete weather independence and continuous high thermal power.
  • Cons: High biomass consumption rates and risk of localized overheating without precise mixing.

Design Alternative 3

Hybrid Solar-Biomass Dryer with Zeolite Regulation: A dual-source thermal system combining a solar air heater and a biomass furnace, integrated with a secondary zeolite adsorption bed to buffer moisture levels[cite: 2].

  • Pros: Weather-resilient, highly energy-efficient, and prevents over-drying spikes using solid-state desiccant buffering[cite: 2].
  • Cons: Higher initial system design complexity.

Selected Final Design

Design Alternative 3 was chosen as the optimal architecture. It integrates a solar thermal air collector, an auxiliary biomass furnace, and a 5 kg secondary zeolite bed to deliver consistent thermal performance and moisture regulation across all operational cycles[cite: 2].

Methodology & Tools (e.g., SolidWorks, CFD Analysis, calculations)

  • SolidWorks & AutoCAD: Utilized for 3D modeling, sheet metal layouts, and mechanical assembly drafting[cite: 2].
  • Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD): Deployed to simulate internal velocity vectors, pressure drops, and thermal distribution patterns[cite: 2].
  • Thermodynamic Modeling: Hand-calculated psychrometric state changes, mass flow rates, and specific energy consumption metrics[cite: 2].

Results & Performance Validation

Performance testing and computational models confirmed that the system achieves an operational Specific Energy Consumption (SEC) of approximately 2,502.58 kJ/kg of evaporated water[cite: 2], paired with uniform airflow distribution across the drying chamber trays validated via CFD[cite: 2].

Conclusions & Recommendations

REZIDAC successfully bridges the gap between renewable energy harvesting and reliable crop processing[cite: 2]. Future upgrades should incorporate automated IoT sensor feedback for real-time valve control and automated zeolite regeneration cycles.
