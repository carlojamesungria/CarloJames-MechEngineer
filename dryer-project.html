Executive Summary

Agricultural sectors face severe post-harvest losses due to weather unpredictability, high ambient humidity, and inefficient drying options. REZIDAC is an engineered agricultural drying system featuring direct solar collection architecture. This project transforms raw agricultural thermodynamic requirements into a robust mechanical system, optimizing natural convective flow, preventing product over-drying, and achieving a calculated 36.2% ROI within the first year.

Problem Statement

Traditional open-sun drying approaches leave agricultural yields heavily exposed to sudden precipitation, environmental contaminants, and uncontrolled moisture fluctuations leading to spoilage or over-drying. Conventional mechanical alternatives often suffer from excessive specific energy consumption and heavy reliance on continuous fossil fuels or grid power. A cost-effective solar-driven configuration is required to maintain clean thermal output while minimizing operational complexity.

Project Background & Context

During peak harvesting seasons, local farmers experience heavy economic losses because open-sun drying leaves crops susceptible to sudden rain, contamination, and fluctuating humidity. REZIDAC was conceptualized to provide a streamlined, localized, and energy-efficient post-harvest processing unit focusing primarily on direct solar thermal capture.

Objectives

  • Design a direct-mode solar agricultural dryer to maximize renewable energy utilization.
  • Optimize thermal efficiency and structural integrity through advanced CFD simulations.
  • Ensure strong economic viability with a rapid payback period and high return on investment.
  • Keep manufacturing simple and cost-effective using locally available components.

Design Constraints & Criteria

  • Thermal Management: Must effectively capture and retain solar radiation to maintain target drying temperatures.
  • Material Availability: Must utilize locally accessible fabrication components to support low-cost manufacturing.
  • Operational Simplicity: Must minimize mechanical moving parts to reduce maintenance overhead.

Design Alternative 1 (Selected)

Direct-Mode Open Solar Dryer: A purely solar-driven configuration utilizing flat-plate collectors to direct ambient air straight into the drying chamber without supplementary heaters.

  • Pros: Zero fuel cost; simple architecture; highly sustainable.
  • Cons: Vulnerable to heavy cloud cover, rain, and nocturnal weather changes.

Design Alternative 2

Standalone Biomass-Fired Forced Convection Dryer: A system powered exclusively by a biomass combustion furnace and a forced convection blower.

  • Pros: Complete weather independence and continuous high thermal power.
  • Cons: High biomass consumption rates and risk of localized overheating.

Design Alternative 3

Hybrid Solar-Biomass Dryer with Zeolite Regulation: A dual-source thermal system combining a solar air heater, biomass furnace, and secondary zeolite adsorption bed[cite: 2].

  • Pros: Weather-resilient and precise humidity control[cite: 2].
  • Cons: Higher initial system design complexity and maintenance load.

Selected Final Design

Design Alternative 1 (Direct-Mode Open Solar Dryer) was chosen as the optimal final architecture. By prioritizing a clean, solar-thermal collector layout, the system eliminates external fuel dependencies while maintaining low manufacturing overhead and achieving an attractive 36.2% ROI.

Methodology & Tools (e.g., SolidWorks, CFD Analysis, calculations)

  • SolidWorks & AutoCAD: Utilized for 3D modeling, component layout, and mechanical assembly drafting.
  • Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD): Deployed to simulate internal velocity vectors, pressure drops, and thermal distribution patterns.
  • Thermodynamic Modeling: Hand-calculated psychrometric state changes, mass flow rates, and specific energy consumption metrics.

Results & Performance Validation

Performance testing and computational models confirmed that the system achieves an operational Specific Energy Consumption (SEC) of approximately 2,502.58 kJ/kg of evaporated water, paired with uniform airflow distribution across the drying chamber trays validated via CFD.

Conclusions & Recommendations

REZIDAC successfully demonstrates the feasibility of clean solar agricultural processing. Future upgrades should incorporate automated tracking systems or supplemental auxiliary heating for high-humidity operational windows.
