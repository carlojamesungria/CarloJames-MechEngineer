--- ## Executive Summary Post-harvest losses remain a critical vulnerability for local agricultural systems due to weather unpredictability and inefficient traditional drying practices. **REZIDAC** is a hybrid biomass-solar agricultural dryer integrated with solid-state zeolite moisture regulation to stabilize drying rates and preserve crop quality. Incorporating physical prototyping, thermodynamic evaluation, and fluid flow validation, the system achieves a strong economic return on investment (ROI) of **36.2%** within its first year, demonstrating high technical feasibility and economic viability for agricultural communities. ## Problem Statement Traditional drying approaches (such as open-sun drying) leave agricultural yields heavily exposed to sudden precipitation, environmental contaminants, and uncontrolled moisture fluctuations leading to spoilage or over-drying. Conventional mechanical dryers often suffer from excessive specific energy consumption and heavy reliance on continuous fossil fuels or grid power. A resilient, cost-effective hybrid drying mechanism is required to maintain consistent thermal output and relative humidity control regardless of ambient weather shifts. ## Project Background & Context Agricultural sectors frequently experience heavy financial losses during peak harvest seasons due to insufficient weather-resilient post-harvest processing infrastructure. REZIDAC was developed to resolve these challenges by coupling solar thermal energy harvesting with a backup biomass furnace, supplemented by a regenerative zeolite adsorption bed for precision humidity control. ## Objectives * **Primary Objective:** Engineer and validate a hybrid biomass-solar agricultural dryer equipped with a zeolite matrix to minimize post-harvest losses and optimize drying efficiency. * **Secondary Objectives:** * Maintain target drying chamber thermal thresholds under varying weather conditions. * Regulate internal vapor pressure deficits to prevent crop case-hardening. * Validate structural and fluid behavior via computational tools prior to physical deployment. * Achieve a high financial return with a fast payback period (targeting an ROI over 35%). ## Design Constraints & Criteria * **Thermal Constraints:** Must sustain operation during low-insolation periods via the biomass furnace backup. * **Manufacturing Criteria:** Must utilize locally available fabrication materials to keep costs low and support local manufacturability. * **Psychrometric Criteria:** Internal air velocity and relative humidity must be maintained at optimal thresholds across stacked product trays. ## Design Alternative 1 * **Direct-Mode Open Solar Dryer:** A purely solar-driven configuration utilizing flat-plate collectors to direct ambient air straight into the drying chamber without supplementary heaters. * *Pros:* Zero fuel cost; simple architecture. * *Cons:* Entirely vulnerable to cloud cover, rain, and nocturnal humidity swings. ## Design Alternative 2 * **Standalone Biomass-Fired Forced Convection Dryer:** A system powered exclusively by a biomass combustion furnace and a forced convection blower. * *Pros:* Complete weather independence and continuous high thermal power. * *Cons:* High biomass consumption rates and risk of localized overheating without precise mixing. ## Design Alternative 3 * **Hybrid Solar-Biomass Dryer with Zeolite Regulation (Selected Design):** A dual-source thermal system combining a solar air heater and a biomass furnace, integrated with a secondary zeolite adsorption bed to buffer moisture levels. * *Pros:* Weather-resilient, highly energy-efficient, and prevents over-drying spikes using solid-state desiccant buffering. * *Cons:* Higher initial system design complexity. ## Evaluation & Trade-Off Analysis *(to compare your alternatives against your criteria)* | Evaluation Criteria | Alternative 1: Open Solar | Alternative 2: Standalone Biomass | Alternative 3: Hybrid + Zeolite (Selected) | | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | | **Weather Independence** | Low | High | **High** | | **Energy Efficiency** | High (Free solar) | Low (High fuel load) | **Optimized (Blended sources)** | | **Humidity Control Precision** | Poor | Moderate | **High (Zeolite-regulated)** | | **Economic Viability** | Moderate | Moderate | **High (36.2% ROI)** | ## Selected Final Design The **Hybrid Solar-Biomass Dryer with Zeolite Regulation (Alternative 3)** was chosen as the final system architecture. By blending solar-heated air with auxiliary biomass heat and routing the stream through a $5\text{ kg}$ zeolite bed[cite: 2], the system guarantees steady drying air parameters and protects product integrity across all operational cycles. ## Methodology & Tools *(e.g., SolidWorks, CFD Analysis, calculations)* * **CAD Modeling:** Detailed 3D assemblies and sheet metal fabrication drawings created using **SolidWorks** and **AutoCAD**. * **CFD Analysis:** Computational Fluid Dynamics simulations deployed to evaluate internal velocity contours, pressure distributions, and thermal profiles inside the plenum. * **Thermodynamic Calculations:** Mathematical balancing of heat transfer rates, mass flow requirements, and specific energy consumption (SEC). ## Results & Performance Validation * **Thermodynamic Performance:** Successfully elevated ambient air to target drying thresholds while stabilizing relative humidity swings. * **Specific Energy Consumption:** Achieved an operational SEC of approximately **$2,502.58\text{ kJ/kg}$ of evaporated water**[cite: 2]. * **Economic Analysis:** Confirmed an attractive **36.2% Return on Investment (ROI)** within the first operational year. * **CFD Flow Validation:** Verified smooth, uniform airflow distribution with minimal dead zones inside the drying chamber. ## Conclusions & Recommendations * **Conclusions:** REZIDAC successfully bridges the gap between renewable energy utilization and dependable agricultural processing by integrating solar, biomass, and desiccant technologies. * **Recommendations:** Future iterations should incorporate IoT-based sensor automation for real-time monitoring and automated zeolite regeneration scheduling. ## Technical Highlights * Dual-source thermal integration blending solar thermal collectors and biomass combustion. * Solid-state zeolite matrix integration for precision relative humidity control. * Rigorous pre-prototype validation using advanced CFD and thermodynamic formulations. ## Skills Applied * Mechanical and thermal system design (psychrometrics, heat exchangers, fluid machinery). * Computer-Aided Engineering (SolidWorks 3D modeling and CFD simulation). * Capital budgeting, cost-benefit analysis, and engineering economics (ROI calculation).
