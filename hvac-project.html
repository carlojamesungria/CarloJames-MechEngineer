Executive Summary

Rapid urbanization and tourism growth in Tagaytay City have driven an unprecedented surge in commercial building construction, resulting in rising energy consumption despite the area's naturally cooler high-altitude climate. This project engineers an energy-efficient Heating, Ventilation, and Air Conditioning (HVAC) thermal system and comprehensive refrigerant piping layout for a mixed-use three-storey office building located along Akle Street, Kaybagal South. Through rigorous ASHRAE-based cooling load calculations and precise AutoCAD drafting, the total building cooling load was determined to be **43.28 TR (519,420.16 BTU/hr)**, managed via strategic zoning with dual-inverter split and floor-mounted air conditioning units.

Problem Statement

Although Tagaytay City benefits from a temperate cool tropical highland climate (mean annual temperature ~22.7°C compared to Manila's ~27.7°C), commercial establishments face severe energy management challenges. Uncontrolled urbanization, tourist influxes, and dense multi-occupancy schedules (ranging from daily admin operations to high-density events in the third-floor event hall) cause high electricity bills, excessive carbon footprints, and inconsistent indoor thermal comfort. Without a tailored HVAC design prioritizing precise load profiling and moisture mitigation under high humidity (84% average relative humidity), buildings risk severe operational inefficiencies.

Project Background & Context

The project site is situated along Akle Street, Kaybagal South, Tagaytay City (elevation ~650 meters above sea level). The building features a multi-functional architectural layout: the **first floor** houses the reception lobby, guest rooms, admin office, control room, and pantry; the **second floor** contains three professional office rooms and a conference room; and the **third floor** is dedicated to a large event hall, a presidential office, and a secondary conference room.

Objectives

  • Provide a comprehensive AutoCAD layout plan for piping, split-type, and floor-mounted air-conditioning units integrated into the 3-storey building.
  • Calculate specific cooling and heating loads for each floor based on ASHRAE standards, occupancy profiles, and Tagaytay weather data.
  • Select appropriate inverter air-conditioning units based on room dimensions, sensible/latent heat gains, and usage profiles.
  • Incorporate proper ventilation rates and closed-cell elastomeric foam insulation to control high indoor humidity and prevent condensation.

Design Constraints & Criteria

  • Regulatory Compliance: Strict adherence to the Philippine National Building Code (PD 1096), Philippine Green Building Code, PSME Code, and DENR Refrigeration Code.
  • ASHRAE Benchmarks: Integration of ASHRAE Standard 55 (Adaptive Thermal Comfort), ASHRAE Standard 62.1 (Ventilation), and ASHRAE Standard 90.1 (Energy Efficiency).
  • Moisture & Condensation Control: Mitigation of high humidity (84% RH, ~22°C dew point) using vapor-tight elastomeric insulation over seamless copper piping.

First Floor Layout & Design

The first floor serves as the primary gateway for clients and occupants, accommodating the main lobby, pantry, admin office, and control room.

  • Lobby: Equipped with a high-capacity **3.5 HP Floor-Mounted Unit** (₱125,000) to handle open-area foot traffic.
  • Admin & Control Rooms: Each fitted with a **1.0 HP Wall-Mounted Inverter Unit** (₱30,000 each) for precise individual thermal control.
  • Pantry: Serviced by a **1.5 HP Wall-Mounted Unit** (₱45,000) to offset heavy equipment and appliance heat gains.
  • Cooling Load Total: **151,605.84 BTU/hr** (including 10% safety factor), equivalent to **12.63 TOR**.

Second Floor Layout & Design

The second floor is partitioned into three professional private offices and a shared conference room dedicated to daily administrative workflows.

  • Offices 1 & 2: Conditioned via **1.0 HP Wall-Mounted Inverter Units** (₱30,000 each).
  • Office 3 & Conference Room: Fitted with **1.5 HP Wall-Mounted Units** (₱45,000 each) to accommodate higher group occupancy.
  • Cooling Load Total: **74,152.99 BTU/hr** (including 10% safety factor), equivalent to **6.17 TOR**.

Third Floor Layout & Design

The third floor houses high-occupancy and executive areas, featuring a large assembly event hall with a stage, a secondary conference room, and the Office of the President.

  • Event Hall: Serviced by **four (4) 3.5 HP Floor-Mounted Units** (₱500,000 total) to manage intense occupant loads (up to 150 people).
  • Conference Room 2: Equipped with a **1.5 HP Wall-Mounted Unit** (₱45,000).
  • Office of the President: Conditioned via a **1.0 HP Wall-Mounted Unit** (₱30,000).
  • Cooling Load Total: **293,661.33 BTU/hr** (including 10% safety factor), equivalent to **24.47 TOR**.

Methodology & Tools (AutoCAD, ASHRAE standards)

  • AutoCAD Drafting: Developed precise 2D floor plans detailing spatial dimensions, lighting fixtures, and refrigerant piping routes linking indoor Fan Coil Units (FCUs) to outdoor Air-Cooled Condensing Units (ACCUs).
  • ASHRAE Cooling Load Estimation: Applied CLTD/CLF and sol-air temperature methodologies for wall/roof heat transfer, solar heat gain factors (SHGF), and internal heat gains.
  • Equipment Specification & Costing: Compiled a detailed Bill of Materials featuring LG Dual Inverter split and floor-mounted systems totaling **₱955,000** for equipment.

Results & Performance Summary

The system design successfully satisfies the dual requirements of thermal comfort and energy efficiency. By utilizing dual-inverter compressor technology, the cooling units dynamically adjust compressor speeds to match fluctuating room thermal loads, yielding up to 40% energy savings compared to standard non-inverter setups while maintaining strict indoor operative setpoints in compliance with ASHRAE Standard 55.

Conclusions & Recommendations

The HVAC design for the Tagaytay commercial building confirms that zoned inverter systems provide an optimal balance of capital cost, operational reliability, and environmental compliance. Future enhancements should incorporate low-Global Warming Potential (GWP) refrigerants like R-1234yf or CO2 (R-744), integrate solar PV renewable energy sources, and deploy a Building Energy Management System (BEMS) for automated optimization.
